Markets are good at pricing what people trade.
They are bad at pricing what they damage.
I try to close that gap.

I am an applied environmental economist working on air quality, energy transitions, and the costs of environmental harm that go uncounted in market prices. My tools are econometrics, dispersion modeling, and spatial analysis. My questions are about efficiency, fairness, and what good environmental policy actually requires.

Research Areas
💨
Air Pollution & Health

What does pollution actually cost the people who breathe it? I estimate health damages using atmospheric dispersion models and epidemiological dose-response functions, translating physical exposure into welfare losses.

⚖️
Environmental Justice

Environmental costs are not distributed randomly. I examine whether income and racial disparities shape who bears unpriced pollution burdens — and what that means for policy evaluation.

⚡
Energy & Climate Policy

When are clean energy investments privately viable, and when do the public benefits exceed private returns? I evaluate energy transitions with attention to which communities and policy designs actually deliver.
