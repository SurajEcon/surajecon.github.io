New Mexico Tech — Department of CS & Engineering

Teaching economics
to engineers

I teach graduate and undergraduates at a Hispanic-Serving Institution in Socorro, NM. My goal is to make economic reasoning concrete — through real data, applied problems, and browser-based games students can play in class.

Courses

Click a course to see a description and any associated classroom games.

Undergraduate
Principles of Microeconomics
▾

Core undergraduate microeconomics: supply and demand, market equilibrium, consumer and producer theory, market power, externalities, and public goods. Taught with an emphasis on applied examples drawn from energy markets and agricultural economics.

🏪 Double Auction Market In development — Market equilibrium 🏭 Factory & Fishery: The Externality Game In development — Externalities & Coase bargaining
Principles of Macroeconomics
▾

Core undergraduate macroeconomics: GDP, unemployment, inflation, fiscal and monetary policy, the Phillips Curve, and open-economy macroeconomics. Strong emphasis on understanding Federal Reserve policy and interpreting real economic data.

🏛️ Chair the Fed: Monetary Policy Simulation ~30 min — Monetary policy & Phillips Curve → 🐂 Bull or Bear? Market Headlines Game ~25 min — Fed policy, inflation, market reactions → 📊 FOMC: Committee Vote In development — Central bank deliberation
Engineering Economics
▾

Economic analysis for engineering decisions: time value of money, present worth, annual worth, rate of return, benefit-cost analysis, depreciation, and income tax effects. Uses the Chan Park textbook with real engineering project case studies.

📈 The Time Machine: Compound Interest ~15 min — Time value of money →
Managerial Accounting
▾

Accounting for internal business decisions: cost behavior, cost-volume-profit analysis, budgeting, variance analysis, and performance measurement. Focuses on how managers use accounting information to plan, control, and evaluate operations.

Graduate
Engineering Data Analytics (EMGT 5004)
▾

Graduate-level applied statistics and data analytics for engineering managers. Topics include regression analysis, statistical inference, time series, and data visualization in R and Python. Students work with real datasets from engineering and operations contexts.

Outreach & Continuing Education
Money Hacks: Engineering Your Financial Future
▾

A weeklong financial literacy intensive for high school students in NMT's Summer STEM program. Covers personal budgeting, compound interest, investing basics, credit scores, entrepreneurship, and crypto — using hands-on games and simulations throughout. Students leave with a personalized financial blueprint.

💸 Spend or Save?~20 min — Budgeting & opportunity cost → 🐂 Bull or Bear?~25 min — Financial markets → 📈 The Time Machine: Compound Interest~15 min — Investing → 🛍️ Dropship or Flop?~25 min — Entrepreneurship & margin → 📉 Crash or Moon?~25 min — Speculation vs investing → 💳 Credit Score Simulator~30 min — Credit & debt → 🔭 Financial Future Flashforward~30 min — Life planning → 🏆 Final Boss Quiz~30 min — Week review →

All Classroom Games

Every game runs in the browser. No installation, no accounts. Designed for 20–30 minutes of class time. Filter by subject area.

Macroeconomics

Chair the Fed: Monetary Policy Simulation

Students role-play as Fed Chair, setting interest rates each quarter to manage inflation and unemployment across a simulated economy. Makes the Phillips Curve tradeoff visceral before students put numbers to paper.

~30 min Individual Monetary Policy

Macroeconomics — Financial Literacy

Bull or Bear?

Twelve real news headlines. Students predict whether each one sends the market up or down, then see the explanation. Covers Fed decisions, earnings reports, inflation, and geopolitical risk. Streak bonuses create competition.

~25 min Competitive Financial Markets

Financial Literacy

Spend or Save?

Ten rounds of real-life financial decisions — tax refunds, car repairs, bonuses. Choices compound across rounds and a running balance shows how early decisions ripple through later ones.

~20 min Individual Budgeting
Play →

Financial Literacy — Engineering Econ

The Time Machine: Compound Interest

An interactive calculator with sliders for starting amount, age, return rate, and time horizon. A bar chart and cost-of-waiting table make the math visible. The moment students drag the start age from 18 to 28 and watch the number collapse is the whole lesson.

~15 min Individual Time Value of Money

Microeconomics — Financial Literacy

Dropship or Flop?

Ten product scenarios. Students evaluate each on margin, shipping time, and competition, then decide viability. After each pick, the verdict reveals the underlying economics. Teaches profit margin, market saturation, and consumer behavior.

~25 min Individual Entrepreneurship
Play →

Financial Literacy

Crash or Moon?

Eight real historical crypto charts with the final three months hidden. Students draw their prediction then pick crash or moon. The real chart is revealed with context. The lesson: short-term prediction is closer to guessing than analysis.

~25 min Individual Speculation
Play →

Financial Literacy

Credit Score Simulator

Eight decisions across eight years (ages 18–25). A live score gauge updates after each choice. At the car loan stage, a rate table shows what the player's score actually costs them in dollars over five years.

~30 min Individual Credit & Debt

Financial Literacy

Financial Future Flashforward

Five major life decisions — education, housing, transportation, investing, career — each showing their projected net worth impact at age 40. A running total updates with every choice. The end screen shows the cost of each decision vs. the optimal path.

~30 min Individual Life Planning
